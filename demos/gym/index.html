    Your first session, start to finish

    1. Pick a class marked “good first session”

      Lifting Foundations, Kettlebell Basics, Circuits, Boxfit, Mobility, Strong at Sixty and Women’s Lifting Club all suit someone who has never trained. It’s free, whichever you choose.

    2. Come ten minutes early

      Ring the bell at the old ticket window. Your coach will show you the changing rooms, the free lockers and the water fountain.

    3. Go at your own pace

      Every move has an easier version and nobody is timing you. Tell the coach about injuries or worries; it stays between the two of you.

    4. No sales pitch afterwards

      If you’d like to come back, pay £9 a class or look at a membership. If not, that’s fine too.

    Five kinds of class, one colour each

    We colour-code classes like weight plates, so the timetable is quick to read: red for strength, blue for kettlebells, yellow for circuits, green for boxing and white for mobility.

    • Strength

      Barbell Club · Lifting Foundations · Women’s Lifting Club · Strong at Sixty

      Squats, deadlifts and presses, coached properly. Foundations starts with an empty bar; Strong at Sixty uses chairs and boxes, with no getting down on the floor.

    • Kettlebells

      Kettlebells · Kettlebell Basics

      Swings, squats and carries. Forty-five minutes and you’ve worked everything.

    • Circuits

      Circuits

      Ten stations, music on, a bit of puffing. Every station has an easier and a harder version.

    Why the tiles stayed

    Kenmore Row Baths opened in 1936 and closed in 2019, when the council couldn’t pay for a new boiler and filters. Tom worked the last shift.

    In 2022 he and Nadia took on the lease. We drained the main pool, built a timber floor across it and laid rubber on top. The glazed tiles, the iron gallery and the handrails from the steps all stayed, and the depth markers are still painted along the side.

    The lifting platforms sit where the deep end was. People who learned to swim here bring their grandchildren to look.

    “I locked up for the last time on a Sunday in March. Now I open up at quarter past six every morning.”

    Tom Rennie, co-owner
